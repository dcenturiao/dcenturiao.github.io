  <div class="home-paper-title">
    Assessing the Local Cost of Urban Climate Resilience:
    An Analysis of the NYC Financial District and Seaport
    Climate Resilience (FiDi) Master Plan
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    With Caroline Welter.
    <span class="home-paper-status">2025.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    <em>Papers in Regional Science</em>, 104(5), 100110.
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-actions">

    <details class="home-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract</summary>
      <div class="home-abstract-text">
        This study assesses the economic, environmental, and fiscal
        implications of New York City's Financial District and Seaport
        Climate Resilience Master Plan, a proposed investment of
        $5–7 billion over 10–15 years. We combine multi-regional
        input-output modeling with an S-curve framework to simulate
        expenditure timing, economic interactions across New York
        City, and regional spillover effects. The findings suggest
        that the project could generate substantial net economic
        benefits, although environmental impacts may be concentrated
        during particular construction phases. The analysis also
        highlights the importance of intergovernmental coordination
        and offers a framework for evaluating large-scale climate
        adaptation investments before implementation.
      </div>

<div class="home-paper">
  <div class="home-paper-title">
    Regional Economic Impacts of COVID-19 Lockdowns and
    Mitigation Effects of Stimulus Checks and Unemployment Insurance
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    With Caroline Welter, Amir Ferreira Neto, and Fernando Perobelli.
    <span class="home-paper-status">2025.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    <em>Economic Analysis and Policy</em>.
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-actions">

    <details class="home-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract</summary>
      <div class="home-abstract-text">
        This paper develops a framework for estimating the regional
        economic consequences of major disruptions using publicly
        available information. Applying a partial hypothetical
        input-output extraction approach with higher-frequency data,
        we simulate changes in value added and employment in
        Southwest Florida during the COVID-19 pandemic. The results
        reveal a substantial contraction in the second quarter of
        2020, followed by a pronounced recovery in the third quarter
        and a smaller decline in the fourth. Stimulus payments and
        unemployment insurance helped mitigate economic losses but
        did not fully reverse the downturn. Household spending
        behavior, savings decisions, and supply constraints limited
        the effectiveness of income support. The framework provides
        a tool for assessing regional crisis-response policies.
      </div>
    </details>

<div class="home-paper">
  <div class="home-paper-title">
    Land Use and Regulating Ecosystem Services Scenarios for
    the Brazilian Pantanal and Its Surroundings Under Different
    Storylines of Future Regional Development
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    With coauthors.
    <span class="home-paper-status">2025.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    <em>Conservation Science and Practice</em>, e70012.
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-actions">

    <details class="home-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract</summary>
      <div class="home-abstract-text">
        This study uses process-based modeling to explore how
        alternative development pathways could affect land use
        and ecosystem services in the Brazilian Pantanal and
        surrounding highlands by 2050. The simulations examine
        soil erosion, sediment production, nutrient retention,
        water quality, and carbon storage. Compared with a
        business-as-usual scenario, a sustainability-oriented
        pathway could reduce soil losses and sediment yield by
        approximately 45%, improve nutrient retention by 2%,
        and increase above-ground carbon storage by 7%.
        Conversely, accelerated human-driven land-use changes
        could increase soil loss by 8% and sediment yield by
        11%, while reducing nutrient retention and carbon
        storage. These findings demonstrate how alternative
        development strategies can influence the long-term
        provision of ecosystem services.
      </div>
    </details>

  <div class="home-paper-title">
    Impacts of Road Transport Infrastructure Investments
    on the Latin American Integration Route
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    With Mateus Boldrine Abrita, Angelo Rondina Neto,
    Ana Paula Camilo, Rafaella Stradiotto Vignandi,
    Guilherme Espíndola Junior, Vanessa Weber,
    Nelagley Marques, and Ruberval Franco Maciel.
    <span class="home-paper-status">2024.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    <em>Regional Science Policy &amp; Practice</em>,
    16(8), 100061.
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-actions">

    <details class="home-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract</summary>
      <div class="home-abstract-text">
        This paper evaluates the short-term economic consequences
        of road infrastructure investments associated with the
        Latin American Integration Route in Mato Grosso do Sul,
        Brazil. Using structural impact analysis and three
        alternative investment scenarios, we estimate the effects
        on regional GDP, productive sectors, and municipalities.
        The simulations suggest that these investments can
        stimulate economic activity, although the benefits differ
        substantially across sectors and locations, with Campo
        Grande emerging as an important beneficiary. Transport
        infrastructure spending also exhibits relatively limited
        geographic dispersion compared with other investments.
        Methodologically, the study incorporates S-curve investment
        scheduling and spatial location quotients to estimate
        project expenditure patterns and distribute their impacts
        geographically, supporting more spatially informed
        public investment planning.
      </div>
    </details>

  <div class="home-paper-title">
    Dynamics of Local Productive Arrangements in the
    Municipalities of Mato Grosso do Sul Considering
    the Transformations of the Bioceanic Corridor
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    With Mateus Boldrine Abrita, Rafaella Stradiotto Vignandi,
    Angelo Rondina Neto, Ana Paula Camilo Pereira,
    Guilherme Espíndola Junior, Nelagley Marques,
    Vanessa Aparecida de Moraes Weber, and Ruberval Franco Maciel.
    <span class="home-paper-status">2023.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    <em>PLOS ONE</em>, 18(4), e0284023.
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-actions">

    <details class="home-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract</summary>
      <div class="home-abstract-text">
        This paper examines how the emerging Bioceanic Corridor
        may influence local productive arrangements in Mato Grosso
        do Sul, Brazil. The corridor is designed to improve
        transportation links between the region and ports in
        northern Chile, potentially shortening trade routes
        between South America and Asia. Using spatial econometric
        methods, the study identifies patterns of productive
        specialization and analyzes the regional economic
        opportunities associated with the new logistics network.
        The findings indicate that the corridor could support
        economic development and competitiveness. However,
        realizing these benefits requires coordinated public
        policies and effective regional integration. Without
        appropriate planning, the corridor may reinforce
        existing geographic inequalities rather than promote
        broadly distributed development.
      </div>
    </details>

<div class="home-paper">
  <div class="home-paper-title">
    Identification and Analysis of the Evolution
    of Local Productive Arrangements
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    With Caroline Welter and Luiz Cypriano.
    <span class="home-paper-status">2021.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-authors">
    <em>Journal of Regional Analysis &amp; Policy</em>,
    51(2), 49–66.
  </div>

  <div class="home-paper-actions">

    <details class="home-abstract">
      <summary>Abstract</summary>
      <div class="home-abstract-text">
        This study identifies and examines changes in local
        productive arrangements across the extractive and
        manufacturing industries of Paraná, Brazil, between
        2006 and 2016. The empirical strategy combines a
        normalized concentration index with exploratory spatial
        data analysis, including global and local Moran's I
        statistics. The analysis identifies 57 industrial
        productive arrangements spanning 23 economic sectors,
        particularly concentrated in the Curitiba Metropolitan
        Region and North Central Paraná. The results indicate
        continued consolidation and development of these
        industrial clusters over the period, especially around
        Curitiba and Londrina. Prominent sectors include food
        and beverages, textiles and clothing, wood and furniture,
        machinery and equipment, rubber, and plastics.
      </div>
    </details>
